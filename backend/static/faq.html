Frequently Asked Questions

Does a high clickbait score mean the article is false?

No. Clickbait, sensationalism, and negative emotional tone are linguistic signals about how an author framed the text. While misleading articles often use sensational rhetoric, true reporting can also occasionally use urgent language. TruthLens evaluates factual truth through external evidence corroboration.

Where does TruthLens get its evidence citations?

TruthLens queries verified public knowledge stores, open encyclopedias (Wikipedia), and fact-checking databases (Google Fact Check Tools API). The system never fabricates citations; if no corroborating records exist, claims are honestly marked UNVERIFIED.

Is TruthLens free to use?

Yes. TruthLens is completely open-access and free for researchers, journalists, educators, and the public.

How is the credibility score calculated?

The credibility score is a deterministic composite calculation combining claim-level evidence support (+), factual contradictions (-), source authority weightings, and linguistic risk penalties. No random numbers are used.
