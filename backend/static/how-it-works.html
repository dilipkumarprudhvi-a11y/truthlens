How TruthLens Verifies Information

TruthLens is designed around a multi-stage, evidence-first methodology that completely decouples rhetorical style from factual truth.

Stage 1: Declarative Claim Extraction

When an article or statement is submitted, the system splits content into syntactic sentences and filters out subjective opinions, rhetorical questions, and greetings to extract atomic testable factual claims.

Stage 2: Real Evidence Retrieval

For each extracted claim, TruthLens generates targeted keyword queries and queries authoritative public knowledge repositories, including Wikipedia, DuckDuckGo Knowledge Index, and the Google Fact Check Tools database. TruthLens adheres strictly to the rule: never fabricate evidence citations.

Stage 3: Natural Language Inference (NLI) & Refutation Matching

The system evaluates retrieved evidence snippets for direct fact-check classifications (False, True, Misleading), refutation markers (e.g. debunked, hoax, disproved), and affirmation markers (e.g. peer-reviewed, confirmed, documented).

Stage 4: Deterministic Scientific Verdict

Claims and evidence are aggregated into four scientific categories:

  • SUPPORTED: Corroborated by independent, authoritative reporting.
  • CONTRADICTED: Refuted or disputed by verified evidence.
  • MIXED: Content combines substantiated facts with disputed assertions.
  • UNVERIFIED: Insufficient independent evidence found in indexed knowledge repositories.

Stage 5: Linguistic Risk Signals

In parallel, the linguistic engine computes 6 deterministic stylistic vectors: sentiment polarity, fear index, clickbait score, partisan balance ratio, Flesch readability, and syntactic formality. These provide critical context on how content is framed.
